# Listening Notes

## Listening setup

For the detailed comparisons below:
- Hardware: Redmi K-Pad connected to Redmi Buds 8 Pro.
- Earphone setting: 均衡听感; noise-cancellation mode, with noise cancellation enabled.
- Device volume: 80%.
- Software: Poweramp Equalizer.

The comparisons were made among no PEQ, V1 PEQ, and V2-A PEQ.

## V1 baseline

The listener reported that V1 (192 kHz-derived profile) offered:
- Slightly fuller bass than the earlier 48 kHz profile.
- Clear mid/high frequencies with controlled harshness.
- A perceived increase in airiness.
- Good balance between vocals and instruments.
- No obvious overall loudness reduction compared with the earlier version.

## V2-A initial cannon-effect test

After the Filters 1–7 gain changes, the listener reported:
- The cannon sound had a more defined, compact explosive core.
- The sound seemed to expand outward into a larger, less dense sphere.
- The perceived result felt more consistent with the auditory impression of artillery fire.

## Detailed listening comparisons

### Test 1 – Hotel California

- Recording: *Hotel California*, 1976 CD version, first track, 1-bit DSD64, 5645 kbps, DFF, DSD_MSBF.
- Listening segment: 00:00–00:50.
- Focus: bass extension and decay.

**No PEQ:** The bass tail lasted longer than with either PEQ. After the bass had decayed to a certain level, it seemed as though it should have stopped, but it lingered a little longer before stopping.

**V1 PEQ:** No clear difference from V2-A could be heard. It felt as though the decay of certain notes was slightly faster.

**V2-A PEQ:** The bass had sufficient quantity. Each bass note was accompanied by an approximately one-second tail that gradually decayed and then stopped.

### Test 2 – Liu Ruoying, “后来” (Later)

- Recording: 24-bit / 48 kHz, 1632 kbps FLAC.
- Listening segment: 1:30–3:00.
- Focus: extension and decay of the bass accompanying the drum beats.

**No PEQ:** The bass accompanying the drum beats lingered relatively long, similar to Test 1. Because the intervals between drum beats were short, the tails overlapped and sounded like a continuous low-frequency background.

**V1 PEQ:** The bass accompanying the drum beats still lingered relatively long, but its level was reduced considerably. The continuous low-frequency background formed by overlapping tails was therefore much quieter than with no PEQ.

**V2-A PEQ:** Although V2-A applies less attenuation in the low-frequency region than V1, it sounded better than V1 in this test. The recovery of each bass note could be heard clearly, and there was basically no continuous low-frequency background formed by overlapping tails.

### Test 3 – Dvořák Cello Concerto / Silent Woods

- Recording: HD EMI, *Dvořák: Cello Concerto – Silent Woods* (2011 Remaster), CD Track 1, “Cello Concerto in B Minor, B.191, Op.104: 1. Allegro”.
- Format: 24-bit / 96 kHz, 2912 kbps FLAC.
- Listening segment: 3:50–5:30.
- Focus: cello resonance and the low bass in the background.

**No PEQ:** The instruments had sufficient low-frequency quantity and sounded slightly dark, with a little rumbling.

**V1 PEQ:** The low frequencies decayed relatively quickly, resulting in insufficient resonance. The instrument's center of gravity seemed higher, as if the instrument had been lifted off the stage floor while being played.

**V2-A PEQ:** Low-frequency resonance was retained without making the sound feel dark. However, the instrument's center of gravity still seemed slightly high.

### Test 4 – Bach Cello Suites, Disc 1

- Recording: EMI Classics, *Bach Cello Suites – Disc 1*, Suite No. 1 in G major, BWV 1007 (2011 Digital Remaster), “Prelude”.
- Format: 1-bit DSD64, 5645 kbps, DFF, DSD_MSBF.
- Listening segment: 00:00–01:22.
- Focus: resonance and decay of the low notes during the solo cello passage.

**No PEQ:** The low-frequency quantity was sufficient, without a rumbling sensation. The resonance was just right.

**V1 PEQ:** The low frequencies were insufficient. Compared with no PEQ, the instrument seemed farther away from the listener.

**V2-A PEQ:** The low frequencies were slightly lower than with no PEQ, but the instrument did not seem farther away.

## Broader listening after the detailed tests

In the following days, the listener also auditioned other CDs, including recordings featuring trumpet, violin, and cello, Chinese rock music from the 1990s, Mandarin pop songs, and Chinese folk songs.

No detailed A/B/C comparisons were made for these additional recordings. The listener made an overall impression-based assessment instead.

For most of this music, there was not much content in either the very low bass or the very high treble. As a result, no PEQ, V1 PEQ, and V2-A PEQ did not sound noticeably different most of the time.

The differences identified in the four detailed tests above remain the differences the listener can clearly perceive at this point.

## Current usage decision

The listener intends to use V2-A PEQ as the default PEQ for the Redmi Buds 8 Pro + Poweramp setup.

This records the listener's current personal preference. It is not a claim that V2-A is objectively superior for every listener, recording, or playback setup.
